# Resume patterns

What strong engineer resumes do, from hiring-side guides (sources at the
end). The resume-writer reads this with STYLE.md: STYLE.md is how you sound,
this file is how the page works. Examples are invented.

## Bullets

1. **Result first, then how.** "Cut onboarding for new data sources from 3
   weeks to 4 days by building a schema-mapping layer." Not "Responsible for
   the ingestion pipeline." (XYZ formula as a thinking check, not a fixed
   sentence) [P1, P2]
2. **Numbers with context.** Before and after, team or customer count,
   adoption: "adopted by 14 teams; deploys 40 to 8 minutes", not "widely
   adopted". [P3]
3. **What, for whom, did it work.** Plain words: "Built a claims-triage agent
   for a regional insurer's ops team; it now handles most intake." [P4]
4. **Your own part in team work.** "Designed the migration plan and moved 30
   services myself", not "Team migrated to Kubernetes." [P5]
5. **Responsibility and accomplishment per role.** One line of scope, then
   results. [P5]
6. **Customer-facing work ends in business results**, not "supported calls":
   deals won, adoption, cost or time saved. [P6]
7. **Field work that became product.** "Turned 3 one-off customer
   integrations into a reusable connector now in the core product." [P7, P9]
8. **Interview-proof.** Every bullet is something you can talk about for ten
   minutes; nothing you cannot defend. [P2]
9. **Visible growth.** Bullets climb across roles: built a feature, then
   owned a system, then led across teams. [P4]
10. **Active verbs, no "-ing" openings, no "results-driven" style filler.**
    [P3, P2]

## Structure

1. **Summary: 2 to 3 plain, factual lines.** What you build, your
   specialty, your setting and scope. No buzzwords; put the 2 or 3 strongest
   facts here. [P2, P8]
2. **One company heading for several roles there**, each title with its
   dates, so the progression shows. [P8]
3. **3 to 6 bullets per role, most relevant first**; older roles 1 to 2.
   [P6, P2, P10]
4. **Short, honest skills line** on page 1; name the stack inside bullets
   too; no tool inventories, no proficiency bars. [P11, P3]
5. **Single column, easy to scan, little bold**, one page unless very
   senior; tailored to the role. [P11, P10]
6. **Context for less-known companies and links** ("Series B, 2M users");
   no bare links. [P8, P3]

## For forward deployed and solutions roles

What postings ask for: owning delivery end to end inside the customer's
environment (prototype to production, not demos), production LLM and agent
work (prompting, agents, evals, MCP), turning repeated field work into
tools and product, feeding findings back to product, and communicating with
users up to executives. Show each of these with evidence. Avoid sounding
like sales instead of engineering. [P12, P13, P7]

## Avoid

Duties instead of outcomes; bullets with no numbers; buzzword summaries;
tool lists you cannot defend; decorative templates and heavy bold; hidden
keyword tricks; titles bigger than the scope shown; bare links. [P5, P3, P2,
P6, P4]
